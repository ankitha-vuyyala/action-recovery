# Proofs {#sec:proofs}

Throughout, a policy is a function from the client's history to its next action, worlds are drawn from a prior, and $N_\ell(t)$ is the number of standing effects of operation $\ell$ at time $t$.

**Proposition 1.** *(i) Prevention with a durable key.* Let $\kappa$ be a function of durable state, so that every attempt of $\ell$, before and after any crash, carries the same $\kappa$. Let $e_1$ be the time of the first execution of any attempt. By the key contract, the service records $\kappa$ at $e_1$, and every execution with key $\kappa$ in $[e_1, e_1 + r)$ creates no new effect. If every execution of every attempt falls in that interval, exactly one effect is created. Re-issuing until an acknowledgement or a refusal arrives ends with that effect acknowledged, provided each attempt is delivered with positive probability. With a known bound $\Delta$, every execution falls in $[t_1, t_{\text{last}} + \Delta]$, so $t_{\text{last}} - t_1 + \Delta < r$ suffices. Because the loop has no time bound, prevention holds with the probability that the loop finishes before this condition fails, and with certainty only if the number of attempts is capped.

*(i′) Retention.* An execution at $e > e_1 + r$ is treated as new. In a world where $a_1$ executed at $e_1$ and its acknowledgement was lost, a late execution after $e_1 + r$, whether of a re-issue or of a delayed original, creates a second effect. After that point the client must resolve the outcome by another attribution source before re-issuing, which is the situation of Propositions 2 and 3. State-based expiry is the same argument with the state change in place of $e_1 + r$.

*(ii) Failure with an in-memory key.* Let the client crash after dispatching attempt $a_1$ with key $\kappa_1$ and before recording it. After the restart a fresh key $\kappa_2 \neq \kappa_1$ is drawn. In a world in which $a_1$ is delayed and both $a_1$ and the re-issued $a_2$ execute, the service sees two distinct keys and creates two effects.

*(iii) Without a key.* This is Li's Proposition 1 [@li2026limbo], restated in my notation. Take two worlds that agree up to time $t$: in $w_1$, attempt $a_1$ never executes; in $w_2$, it executes at $t + \epsilon$. They are observation-equivalent up to $t$. A policy that re-issues at or before $t$ duplicates in $w_2$, and a policy that never re-issues leaves $\ell$ missing in $w_1$. If $\Delta$ is unknown, such a $t$ exists for every waiting time. ∎

**Proposition 2.** Consider one round, and let $t^\star = t_{\text{last}} + \Delta + \lambda$.

1. By (d), every attempt dispatched up to $t_{\text{last}}$ has executed by $t_{\text{last}} + \Delta$ or never will.
2. By (b) and (c), a read at $t^\star$ returns exactly the standing effects that carry $\tau$ and executed before $t^\star - \lambda$, which by step 1 are all that will ever exist from these attempts.
3. By (f) and (g), the effects that carry $\tau$ are exactly the standing effects of $\ell$, and no other actor removes them. The count read at $t^\star$ therefore equals the eventual count of the attempts made so far.

If $N = 0$, no attempt executed and none is pending, so a re-issue under the same $\tau$ cannot race an earlier attempt, and a new round begins. If $N > 1$, targeted compensation of $N - 1$ effects chosen by id leaves exactly one, using (e). By (e) a compensation may be retried through the faulty channel until a further read, after another lag $\lambda$, confirms $N = 1$. If $N = 1$, the policy stops. By (a), $\tau$ survives crashes, so any history resumes the same procedure without creating an attempt it cannot attribute. Each round re-issues at most once, and a round ends without a re-issue with probability at least the probability that its attempt executes, so the number of rounds is finite with probability one if that probability is positive. No effect of another actor is touched, because only effects that carry $\tau$ are compensated and, by (g), they all belong to $\ell$.

For the bound, drop (d). A round that ends at horizon $H$ can leave an inconsistent state only if some attempt executes after $H - \lambda$: otherwise steps 2 and 3 hold at $H$. For attempt $i$, dispatched at $t_i$, this requires that it executes at all and that $\delta_i > H - \lambda - t_i$, which has probability $p_i(1 - F(H - \lambda - t_i))$ if delays are independent of whether the attempt executes. A union bound over the attempts gives the stated bound. ∎

**Proposition 3.** If effects cannot be neutralised, $N_\ell = 1$ at quiescence implies that exactly one execution happened. That is prevention, so Proposition 1(iii) applies whenever $\Delta$ is unknown. If $\Delta$ is known, waiting until $t_{\text{last}} + \Delta + \lambda$ before the count makes $N = 0$ equivalent to "no attempt executed or pending" (Proposition 2, steps 1–3), so a re-issue in that case cannot create a second execution. A transport redelivery is an execution that no client action caused and that no client action can prevent: in a world with redelivery and one without, the client's history up to the first execution is the same, and without a key the service accepts both executions. With a tag, the client can detect the second effect and report it. ∎

**Proposition 4.** In $w_1$ the client’s attempt executed and created effect $e$. In $w_2$ it did not, and another actor created $e'$ with the same content. A content read returns one matching effect in both worlds, and every other observation is identical, so the two worlds are observation-equivalent while $N_\ell = 1$ in $w_1$ and $N_\ell = 0$ in $w_2$. A content-only policy takes the same action in both worlds. If it skips, $\ell$ is missing in $w_2$. If it re-issues, $\ell$ is duplicated in $w_1$. If it re-issues and then removes one of the two matching effects, it either removes its new effect, which leaves $\ell$ missing in $w_2$, or removes the earlier match, which in $w_2$ is $e'$ (collateral). Either way it is wrong in $w_2$. With a tag, $e$ carries $\tau$ and $e'$ does not, so a read by tag distinguishes the two worlds. ∎

**Proposition 5.** *If.* With exclusive writing, the aggregate changes only through the client's own effects, so $N_\ell = (A(t) - A(t_0))/u$, where $u$ is the unit of the operation, $A$ the aggregate and $A(t_0)$ its value read before the first attempt; $\ell$ is attributable. *Worst case.* Suppose another actor may add one unit of the same size during the window. Then a world in which the client’s attempt executed and the other actor's did not, and the reverse world, give the same aggregate. They are observation-equivalent under an aggregate read, while $N_\ell$ differs, so any decision about re-issuing or compensating is wrong in one of them. A fungible compensation removes "a unit" and cannot tell whose. ∎

**Proposition 6.** Let the client crash after dispatching $a_1$ and before recording its outcome. After the restart, compare a world in which the crash happened just before the dispatch, so that $\ell$ never ran, with one in which it happened just after, so that $\ell$ may have executed. Without a durable trace of the dispatch and without a recoverable tag, the persisted state is the same in both. A policy that issues $\ell$ duplicates it in the second world if $a_1$ executed. A policy that does not issue leaves it missing in the first. A policy that treats $\ell$ as possibly dispatched and resolves it by reading needs a tag it can count by, and an in-memory tag is lost. Conversely, if $\tau$ is recoverable, either from a write-ahead record or deterministically from durable step identity, the policy "treat the step as possibly dispatched, wait $\Delta + \lambda$, count by $\tau$, issue if $N = 0$" is correct in both worlds by Proposition 2. ∎

**Proposition 7.** Fix a history $h$ up to $t_d$ that satisfies the premise. Because $\ell$ is attributable, the last read is exact for effects that executed before $t_d - \lambda$. Among the worlds consistent with $h$, the pending attempt either never executes ($w_0$) or executes after $t_d - \lambda$ ($w_L$); the policy cannot tell them apart. After $t_d$ it takes no action on $\ell$, so the number $k \in \{0, 1\}$ of its other effects of $\ell$ that remain standing is fixed at $t_d$. Here $k = 0$ means that it did not re-issue or that it compensated its re-issue, and $k = 1$ means that it re-issued or kept its re-issue. The final count is $k$ in $w_0$ and $k + 1$ in $w_L$, so $k = 0$ is wrong in $w_0$ and $k = 1$ is wrong in $w_L$. A policy that chooses $k = 1$ with probability $q$ is inconsistent with probability
$$\begin{aligned} &q\Pr[w_L \mid h] + (1 - q)\Pr[w_0 \mid h] \\ &\quad\ge\; \min\{\Pr[w_0 \mid h], \Pr[w_L \mid h]\}. \end{aligned}$$
Settle chooses $k = 1$, so its error at $h$ is $\Pr[w_L \mid h]$, and it attains the minimum if and only if $\Pr[w_L \mid h] \le \Pr[w_0 \mid h]$. If Settle's own re-issue may fail to execute, its error is larger by $\Pr[w_0 \mid h]$ times the probability of that failure. ∎
