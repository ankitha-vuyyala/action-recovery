# Background and related work {#sec:related}

**Exactly-once effects in distributed systems.** Two parties cannot agree on whether a message was acted upon when messages can be lost [@akkoyunlu1975; @gray1978notes]. Practical systems therefore build *effectively-once* behaviour from at-least-once delivery plus deduplication. The ingredients are idempotent operations and request identifiers [@helland2012idempotence], durable completion records that let a server recognise a retried request [@lee2015rifl], transactional logs in stream processors [@wang2021kafka], and exactly-once semantics for stateful serverless workflows [@zhang2020beldi]. Commercial APIs expose the same idea as an idempotency key [@doc_stripe; @doc_tigerbeetle]. Durable-execution engines persist workflow progress but execute each activity at least once, and advise that activities be idempotent [@doc_temporal; @doc_dbos]. When an operation cannot be undone atomically, sagas and compensating transactions restore consistency by running a semantic inverse [@garciamolina1987sagas; @korth1990compensating]. All of these mechanisms assume either that the server deduplicates or that the client knows which effects exist. My setting removes both assumptions and asks what remains. The answer, attribution through a reference the client writes, is close to what payment integrations do with merchant references. My contribution is to state when it suffices and when it cannot, and to measure how it compares with keys and durable execution when an autonomous agent drives the calls.

**Ambiguous tool outcomes in language-model agents.** Sun [@sun2026didithappen] builds observation-equivalent pairs of worlds, in which a write either did or did not execute, and shows that an agent without further information can be right in at most half of them. IdempotencyBench [@gopnal2026idempotencybench] measures idempotency violations under retries with a hidden effect ledger. Li [@li2026limbo] gives the most complete study to date: twelve fault modes on simulated services, nine models and three production harnesses. That study proves that no verification-only policy is exactly-once under late commits without an in-flight bound. It also finds that idempotency keys remove most duplicates, and that a client-side guard helps weaker models more than frontier models. Trofimov and Novikov [@trofimov2026anomalies] model effect histories and name the anomalies that arise at the agent-tool boundary: duplicated, missing, orphaned compensation and residue. They argue that without an authoritative-outcome primitive no protocol is both safe under unknown outcomes and safe to compensate. Their census of tool annotations in the Model Context Protocol shows that such annotations cannot express these properties, and the specification itself calls its idempotency and destructiveness annotations untrusted hints [@doc_mcp]. Wang [@wang2026callability] shows that interfaces which expose effect semantics reduce duplicate effects.

My work accepts Li's impossibility result for *prevention* and studies *convergence* instead. It also differs from this line of work in four respects:

- I *construct* the missing outcome primitive from fields that ordinary APIs already persist, and measure in a census how often such fields exist; Trofimov and Novikov's census covers tool annotations, not the services behind the tools.
- I evaluate on real services rather than simulated ones.
- I add agent-process crashes, a workflow engine, compensation-based baselines, look-alike records from concurrent users and aggregate-only reads.
- I prove a lower bound for policies that must stop at a horizon, which characterises the residual failures I measure.

**Recovery mechanisms for agents.**

- *Verification.* Verify-before-retry wrappers check a postcondition before repeating a call [@mansoor2026verified]. Li's guard adds automatic keys, a re-check after the documented lag and a block on repeats it cannot verify [@li2026limbo].
- *Transactions.* Atomix gates commits on progress, compensates failed branches and deduplicates ambiguous retries with keys [@mohammadi2026atomix]. Cordon offers semantic transactions with an effect outbox [@chen2026cordon]. Agentic Transaction proposes ACID-style guarantees for data agents [@sun2026agentictx].
- *Compensation.* Robust Agent Compensation and SagaLLM bring log-based compensation and sagas to agents [@perera2026rac; @chang2025sagallm].
- *Checkpoint and restore.* AgentRewind [@zhuang2026agentrewind] and DART [@yang2026dart] restore agent state. Rolling back agent state does not undo effects in the world, and a restored agent may re-synthesise slightly different requests that a server treats as new [@zheng2026acrfence]. CapLease measures such equivalent re-proposals and binds them to a durable authorisation state [@xu2026caplease].

Except for Li's guard and verify-before-retry, these mechanisms assume keyed downstream services or detectable failures. I include baselines from the verification family (verify-before-retry and Li's guard), the compensation family (log-based rollback) and durable execution (a Temporal workflow engine), together with idempotency keys, all with matched retry budgets; transactional runtimes and checkpoint-and-restore are not represented. I also add a variant of the guard that waits as long as Settle, so that waiting alone cannot explain a difference.

**Fault injection and reliability benchmarks for agents.** Several benchmarks stress agents with tool failures, timeouts, rate limits or corrupted outputs [@gupta2026reliabilitybench; @sigdel2026toolmisuse; @zhu2026whentoolsfail; @tan2026agentchaos]. Related work studies guards under state races [@zheng2026stale], revisable streaming execution [@zhai2026revisable], and agents that claim success when the state disagrees [@advani2026falsesuccess]. These works measure task success under faults. I measure the correctness of the *final external state* after an ambiguous write, which task success does not capture, and I also score whether the agent's report is honest about that state.
